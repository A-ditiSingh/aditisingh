The model · Content collections

A centralized way to group content the way reps actually search

Research showed a consistent behavior: when results weren't relevant, reps wanted to narrow by broad organizational areas — business units, regions, product lines. The platform had no path for that. Content collections created one. Scroll to see the flow end to end.

Step 01 · Admin
Define collections that mirror the org
Admins create content collections that match how reps in their organization think about content — mapped to sub-orgs, product lines, or regions.
Step 02 · Admin
Map spots into each collection
Admins assign the relevant spots — the folders in the Highspot platform — to each collection, once.
Step 03 · Publisher
Content tags itself
When a publisher adds a piece of content to a spot, it's automatically tagged with the collection that spot belongs to. No extra work at publish time.
Step 04 · Rep
Reps scope, and relevance follows
Reps limit results by content collection, surfacing only the content area most relevant to their role — the payoff of everything upstream.
User insight

Promoting the top 3 collections addresses most scenarios

To keep pre-submission scoping simple, my PM and I returned to users with a focused question: how many collections do reps actually access when searching? Even users with access to many consistently engaged with only 1–3. That set the core principle — make the frequent case easy, keep the rare case possible.

Design implication

Design the dropdown for frequent tasks, filters for scale

The 1–3 insight split the work cleanly across two surfaces.
01 · In the dropdown
Promote scoping to frequent collections
The dropdown limits scoping to the 1–3 collections most relevant to the user, before submit. The dropdown stays clean.
02 · In the filters
Allow filtering to other collections post-search
Defer to filters for the rarely used collections. This scales to the edge cases without cluttering the primary path.
Impact

Scoping lifted click conversion for sellers

+15%
Not every search needs scoping — a rep searching by document name has no reason to narrow. The meaningful measure is whether scoping helps when reps choose it. In organizations with content collections configured, scoped searches produce 15% more clicks on results than unscoped searches.
