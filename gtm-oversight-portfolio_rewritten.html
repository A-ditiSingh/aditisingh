Go to market with Initiatives

Enablement teams can manage, execute, and measure Go-To-Market strategies. They can connect rep activities like content usage and training to business outcomes such as pipeline and revenue — and identify what practices to amplify.

The Problem

Go to market teams are investing without visibility into what's working

Teams invest in strategic initiatives — training programs, plays, conversation frameworks — but have no reliable way to know if any of it is actually working.

There is no reliable way to know whether reps are executing as expected, whether they are having the right conversations, or whether those activities are driving revenue.

GTM teams have no visibility into whether their strategy is driving revenue outcomes.

The Product

A three-phase loop: Define, Configure, Track

The product is structured around the three phases of running an initiative: defining what the initiative is and what it aims to achieve, configuring how success will be measured, and tracking progress against those goals.

The workflow gives teams the information they need to identify which activities to continue investing in and which to adjust.

Learning 01 — Creation

A blank slate and complete flexibility increased the effort of defining the initiative.

The initial design used a WYSIWYG editor, intended to give leaders flexibility in articulating their initiative. In testing with pilot customers, users focused on formatting decisions rather than the strategic content of the initiative.

Feedback from 10 customers in our pilot program made the problem concrete:

7/10

Initiative owners said a blank slate was an overwhelming starting point

3/10

Did not expect to define the initiative in the WYSIWYG editor at all

4/10

Liked the WYSIWYG experience due to familiarity, but needed a template to reduce the number of decisions to be made

BeforeOpen WYSIWYG — leaders design their own layout
AfterStructured drag-and-drop — constrained inputs, clear scaffolding

Learning 02 — Data Engagement

The correlation metric was hard to understand.

The scorecard introduced a metric to answer the question "Is completing an activity correlated with higher revenue outcomes?" The metric was intended to help teams differentiate high and low impact activities.

Customer feedback highlighted that the metric was valuable but hard to understand:

0/10

Customers understood the metric before it was explained to them

10/10

Loved the metric once they understood it

Customer research highlighted that the data was useful but the product wasn't doing a great job of communicating this to the user. The metric was presented in a "Business Outcomes" section, where the revenue figure was the headline and the activity driving that revenue appeared as secondary text below it.

The team addressed the comprehension gap through customer onboarding conversations in the short term, but renaming the section to "Business Drivers" resolved the issue at the product level.

The Pivot — Naming & Framing

Renaming "Business Outcomes" to "Business Drivers" changed how customers read and used the data

Renaming the section from "Business Outcomes" to "Business Drivers" was more than a copy change. The new name shifted the mental model from revenue as the primary lens to activity as the driver of revenue, which required updates to chart labels, the configuration flow, and how the data was presented to users.

A causal chain GTM leaders can act on

The product presents a sequential causal chain that connects rep behavior, customer conversations, and revenue outcomes, giving leaders a structured view of whether their initiative is working and where to take action.

Key Learnings

What this product taught me

Learning 01
Simplifying configuration surfaced usage patterns the team had not anticipated
Simplifying the creation flow surfaced usage patterns the team had not designed for. Removing structural barriers allowed users to focus on the strategic decisions the product was built to support.
Learning 02
How data is framed determines whether users can interpret and act on it
The correlation metric itself did not change between iterations. Changing the section name and the visual hierarchy around the metric significantly improved user comprehension and engagement with the data.
