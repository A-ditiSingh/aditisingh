# Portfolio Writing Style — Aditi Singh

Apply this style whenever writing or editing copy for Aditi's UX portfolio, case studies, project summaries, or professional narrative.

## Voice and Tone

Write in a clear, professional, analytical tone — like a senior product designer documenting decisions for a knowledgeable audience. The reader is a hiring manager, design lead, or PM who values precision over flair.

Do NOT write marketing or editorial copy. Avoid:
- Punchy fragments used for dramatic effect
- Rhetorical pivots ("It wasn't X. It was Y.")
- Poetic or figurative language
- Abstract framing that replaces concrete description

## Sentence Structure

- Use complete sentences with a clear subject, verb, and object
- Name the actual actor: the team, users, research findings, the product — not an abstraction
- Prefer one fuller sentence over two short dramatic ones
- Do not omit the subject for stylistic effect

## Voice Calibration — First Person vs. Descriptive

- Use first person ("I scoped…", "I proposed…", "I updated…") for personal design decisions, judgments, and actions Aditi took
- Use descriptive voice with the product, feature, or component as the subject ("A key differentiator in the scorecard was a metric that correlated activity completion with revenue outcomes") when describing what the product is, contains, or does
- Do not over-use "I" when the sentence is really about the state of the product rather than an action taken

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Prefer |
|---|---|
| "I introduced a metric in the scorecard to answer the question…" | "A key differentiator in the scorecard was a metric that correlated activity completion with revenue outcomes. I placed it in the existing Business Outcomes section…" |

## Language Rules

- Replace metaphor and figurative language with a factual description of what was observed or what happened
- Replace abstract editorial shorthand with a concrete statement of the actual situation
- Replace rhetorical "problem labels" (e.g. "it was a framing problem") with what the evidence actually showed
- Keep register professional and calm — not casual, not dramatic
- Do not restate a point that has already been made clearly in the same section. If two sentences already convey the idea, a third summary sentence is redundant and should be cut

## Structuring a Case Study Section

When writing a section about a design decision or pivot, lead with the operational context before presenting the problem. The reader should understand *what existed and why* before the problem lands.

Order within a section:
1. What was in place, and the reasoning behind it (product context, prior scope, why it was set up that way)
2. What went wrong, supported by concrete evidence (customer feedback, usage data)
3. The decision or pivot made in response
4. The downstream changes the pivot required

Do NOT open a pivot section with the problem — it strands the reader without enough context to judge whether the decision was reasonable.

## Reference Rewrites

Use these as a calibration reference for the expected style:

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Prefer |
|---|---|
| "The gap isn't in strategy. It's in visibility." | "The teams have no visibility into whether their strategy is driving revenue outcomes." |
| "A blank slate created friction where we expected freedom." | "A blank slate and complete flexibility increased the effort of defining the initiative." |
| "That gap — between zero comprehension and universal enthusiasm — wasn't a data problem. It was a framing problem." | "The customer research highlighted that the data was useful but the product wasn't doing a great job of communicating this to the user." |

## Quick Checklist Before Outputting

- [ ] Does every sentence have a named subject?
- [ ] Are there any fragments used for dramatic effect? Remove them.
- [ ] Is there any metaphor or figurative language? Replace with literal description.
- [ ] Are there any short punchy pivots ("It wasn't X. It was Y.")? Rewrite as a single complete sentence.
- [ ] Does the sentence describe what actually happened, not a label for what happened?
- [ ] Are there any redundant summary sentences that restate a point already made? Cut them.
- [ ] Is first person used only for personal decisions, not for describing what the product is?
- [ ] Does each pivot/decision section open with operational context before the problem?
