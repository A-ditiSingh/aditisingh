Improving the content behind AI-generated sales guidance

Highspot AI-assisted workflow

When sellers ask the Agent for next steps, it pulls guidance from sales playbooks. When that content isn't actionable, the quality of recommendations suffers. I designed an evaluation system that helps authors improve their plays so actions are clear for both sellers and the agent.

Role
Design Lead, AI Agent
Stage
Zero to One
Domain

Timeline

Play evaluation panel — single-turn improvement cards in the content viewer

Context

Sellers ask the AI agent what to do next, and the answer depends on the content behind it

Highspot has an AI agent that lives inside active sales opportunities. Sellers ask it what to do next, and its answers draw on company-specific playbooks — competitive intelligence, talk tracks, and recommended actions. When the content is strong, the guidance is specific and contextual. When it isn't, the agent falls back on generic advice.

The design problem was not the agent itself — it was the quality of the content feeding it.

AI agent — seller asking for next steps within an active deal

Problem

Playbooks that aren't upfront about their actions are hard to follow — for humans and AI alike

The problem
Actions buried in playbooks are hard to act on, whether you're a rep reading them or an agent extracting them
When recommended actions are buried in prose, reps have to work to find them. The agent has the same problem — if the actions aren't clear, it can't surface them reliably either.
The opportunity
Evaluating plays based on what the AI can extract creates a feedback loop that improves content for everyone
If we evaluate a play based on the actions the AI can extract, and surface suggestions that would make those actions clearer, the play gets better for reps reading it directly and for the agent surfacing it in a deal.
Approach 1 — Initial direction

Let authors edit the actions the AI extracts from their playbook

The first approach was to let authors directly edit the action items the AI had already extracted — giving them control over exactly what sellers see in the agent. This kept the playbook untouched and gave authors a direct lever over the output.

Prototype — editing extracted action items from the playbook

Feedback from authors

Initial feedback revealed a motivation problem: doing work specifically to improve AI output — work that didn't make the playbook itself better — wasn't something authors had a clear incentive to do. The extra step felt disconnected from their actual job.

Approach 2 — Pivot

Improve the playbook content rather than the extracted actions

Instead of asking authors to do work specifically for the agent, the pivot shifted to improving the playbook itself. A clear, actionable play is better for reps reading it directly and better for the agent extracting from it. The evaluation surfaces focused suggestions that make actions clearer in the source content — framed as a content quality task, not an AI optimization task.

Approach 2 — Three ways to surface suggestions

Exploring three levels of interaction for surfacing improvement suggestions to authors

I explored three levels of interaction — read-only text, structured cards, and free-form chat — all scoped to the content viewer where authors review published content.

Prototype — three proposal options for surfacing improvement suggestions

Decision

Start with static, evolve to single-turn cards

Multi-turn chat was ruled out — the engineering complexity wasn't justified, and building a conversational editor would have moved the product toward a page builder, which wasn't the right direction. The decision was to ship static suggestions first to validate that authors engage with the feedback, then evolve to single-turn cards once that's confirmed.

Version 1 — Static suggestions

Shipping static suggestions within the constraints of the existing authoring experience

The suggestions had to work within what the current content authoring experience allowed, so I explored a few placements and formats to weigh the trade-offs of each. We shipped static suggestions to a small set of customers to validate whether authors engaged with the feedback before investing in a more interactive format.

Prototype — static improvement suggestions in the content viewer

Feedback from customers

Customers found the suggestions too high-level and open-ended. They wanted bite-sized, actionable suggestions they could act on directly, rather than broad direction that still left them to work out the specific change.

Version 2 — Single-turn cards

Pivoting to single-turn cards that accommodate the play's text formatting

In response, we pivoted to single-turn cards. I explored designs that accommodated the range of text formatting a play could contain, so each suggestion could map to a specific, editable change in the source content rather than a general note the author had to interpret.

Prototype — single-turn improvement cards in the content viewer

Takeaways
01

Content quality is a product design problem

When AI surfaces content, the design surface extends beyond the UI into the content itself. Improving seller-facing recommendations meant designing a feedback loop for the authors who create that content — not just the interface sellers see.

02

Structured cards that make the action explicit are more likely to be acted on than static text

Static suggestions are easy to overlook. Cards that make the action explicit and executable within the same panel reduce the effort required to follow through. The format of feedback affects whether it gets acted on.
