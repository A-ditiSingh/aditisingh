Context

What a business initiative is

A business initiative is a coordinated effort across sales, marketing, and enablement to bring a product, feature, or capability to market. It sets the messaging and how the teams work together to drive adoption.

User problem

Initiative owners can't tell if reps are executing, or if it's working

Teams invest in plays, training, and content to run these initiatives, but they have no way to track whether reps are following through, or whether any of it is driving revenue.

Business opportunity

Connecting rep activity to revenue makes Highspot harder to cut

When every software purchase has to justify itself, a platform that connects rep activity to revenue is harder to cut. This moves Highspot from managing content to showing its revenue impact.

Step 1

Driving alignment on what the scorecard should measure

The scorecard was the most contested artefact in the product. I used low-fidelity designs as an alignment tool across GTM and leadership. We agreed on which metrics to include, how to track progress against a goal, and how to organize them as a causal chain (Enablement → Engagement → Business Outcomes) so customers could trace rep behavior through to revenue impact.

Early user feedback

Initiatives were hard to create, and the main metric was hard to understand

Pilot feedback from 10 customers surfaced two areas that needed substantial redesign before launch.

Pivot 01

Reducing the barrier to defining an initiative

The creation experience was built on an existing WYSIWYG editor. Pilot feedback showed users were making formatting decisions instead of focusing on the strategic content of the initiative.

7/10

Initiative owners said a blank slate was an overwhelming starting point

3/10

Did not expect to define the initiative in the WYSIWYG editor at all

4/10

Liked the WYSIWYG experience due to familiarity, but needed a template to reduce the number of decisions they had to make

I replaced the WYSIWYG with a structured drag-and-drop scaffold. The option to add a written overview remained, but was no longer the default.

BeforeOpen WYSIWYG — leaders design their own layout
AfterStructured drag-and-drop — constrained inputs, clear scaffolding, option to add an overview

Business Outcomes section — original framing

Pivot 02

Reframing the main metric for clarity

The scorecard had a metric correlating activity completion with revenue outcomes. I placed it in the Business Outcomes section, but pilot customers couldn't understand it without explanation.

0/10

Customers understood the metric before we explained it to them

10/10

Loved the metric once they understood it

Revenue was the headline metric, and the activity driving it was buried as secondary text. The layout obscured the connection the metric was meant to show.

Reframe

Renaming and reorganizing the sections to host the metric

We created a dedicated "Business Drivers" section to host the metric, giving it the emphasis and framing it needed. The section makes explicit that these are the rep activities correlated with revenue outcomes.

Introducing the "Business Drivers" section
