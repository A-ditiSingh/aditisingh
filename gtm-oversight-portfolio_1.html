Transforming fragmented data into insights

Enablement and marketing teams invest effort to run business initiatives, but have no reliable way to know what is working. I designed the end-to-end product to close that gap.

Role
Lead Product Designer
Timeline
Feb 2025 – Oct 2025
Team

Persona

The Initiative scorecard, a dashboard that shows seller activity and revenue outcomes.

Problem

Go-to-market teams have no visibility into whether their strategy is driving revenue

User problem
Initiative owners cannot tell whether reps are executing or whether it is working
Teams invest in training programs, plays, and conversation frameworks to execute strategic initiatives, but have no reliable way to track whether reps are following through or whether those activities are producing revenue outcomes. The decisions that separate effective initiatives from ineffective ones — what to fix, what to scale, what to cut — cannot be made without that information.
Business opportunity
Closing this gap makes Highspot indispensable when companies are cutting costs
In an environment where companies are scrutinizing every software purchase, customers need to demonstrate that their enablement investment is producing results. A platform that surfaces the connection between rep activity and revenue outcomes gives customers a concrete reason to keep it. Closing this gap moves Highspot from a content management tool to an optimization layer that drives revenue — a harder category to cut.
Zero to One — The Initiative Scorecard

Aligning GTM and leadership on what the scorecard should measure

The Initiative Scorecard was the most contested artefact in the product, so I started here. I used low-fidelity designs and options as a tool for alignment across GTM and leadership, and drove agreement on three things.

Which metrics to include. The scorecard needed to capture percentage of reps who completed each activity, revenue outcomes, and a metric linking activity completion to revenue, showing whether the activities driving the initiative were also driving the deal.

How to track performance against a goal. Each activity needed a goal set by the initiative owner, so the scorecard could show whether execution was on course rather than just reporting raw numbers.

How to organize multiple metrics. The metrics were organized as a causal chain, Enablement, Engagement, Business Outcomes, so customers could read from the leading indicators of rep behavior through to revenue impact.

Zero to One — End to End Flow

Creating a three-phase model for running a go-to-market initiative

The end-to-end flow consolidated into three phases that customers move through for any initiative. Each phase has its own surface, but all three connect through the Initiative object — context set in Define carries into Configure, and the metrics surfaced in Track tie back to the goals set in Configure.

This was an expansive project that evolved significantly from inception to general availability. The case study below presents two design pivots that were informed by customer feedback after the pilot release.

Design Pivots between pilot and launch

Streamlining the creation experience & reframing the correlation metric

The product was first released to a pilot cohort of 10 customers ahead of general availability. Feedback from that cohort surfaced two areas where the design needed substantial change, so the experience at launch reflects the following pivots.

Pivot 01 — Revisiting the creation experience

Reducing the barrier to entry for defining an initiative

The initial version of the Initiative creation experience was built on an existing WYSIWYG editor in the product. Users could drag and drop components to tell the story of the initiative and add relevant content.

Feedback from 10 customers in the pilot indicated that the open-ended format was not working — users focused on formatting decisions rather than the strategic content of the initiative.

7/10

Initiative owners said a blank slate was an overwhelming starting point

3/10

Did not expect to define the initiative in the WYSIWYG editor at all

4/10

Liked the WYSIWYG experience due to familiarity, but needed a template to reduce the number of decisions they had to make

I used that feedback to make the case for replacing the WYSIWYG with a structured drag-and-drop model. In the new structured object, users dragged and dropped relevant content to build the Initiative. The option to add a written overview was still available, but was no longer the default path.

BeforeOpen WYSIWYG — leaders design their own layout
AfterStructured drag-and-drop — constrained inputs, clear scaffolding, option to add an overview

Pivot 02 — Reframing the correlation metric

Reframing the key metric for visibility and clarity

A key differentiator in the scorecard was a metric that correlated activity completion with revenue outcomes. Because revenue was the primary measure of practice success, I placed this metric in the existing Business Outcomes section of the scorecard.

0/10

Customers understood the metric before we explained it to them

10/10

Loved the metric once they understood it

Customer research highlighted that the data was useful but the product wasn't doing a great job of communicating this to the user. I had placed the metric in a "Business Outcomes" section, where the revenue figure was the headline and the activity driving that revenue appeared as secondary text below it.

Reframe

Renaming and reorganizing the sections to host the metric

After feedback from the pilot, together with the leadership and our go-to-market teams, we decided to create a new "Business Drivers" section in the scorecard to host the hero metric.

The new name and a dedicated section in the scorecard gave the metric the emphasis and the space it needed to deliver a clear message: these are the activities that are driving revenue.
