Creating an online catalogue of books from photographs

After two failed attempts to manually catalogue our home library of 1,000+ books, I built an app that converts photos of my bookshelves into a searchable catalogue. I owned the requirements, the design, and the build: a live product with auth, a real database, and AI enrichment.

01 · The Problem

We needed a searchable catalogue of the books in our home library

We have a home collection of 1,000+ books and wanted a way to search through it, mainly to check what we already own when we're at used bookstores. Doing this manually was tedious, and we tried and failed twice before building an app.

02 · Attempt One · 2020

Scanning barcodes, one book at a time

We downloaded Libib, a solid library cataloguing app, and started scanning books one by one. We made it to fifty before giving up and never opening the app again.

Why it failed

Cataloguing 1,000 books one barcode at a time was too slow to ever finish.

03 · Attempt Two · 2023

Translating pictures into an excel sheet

For the second attempt, we used ChatGPT's image processing to photograph our shelves and pull book titles and authors straight into a spreadsheet. Getting the data in was quick, but we couldn't easily access it on the go.

Why it failed

The spreadsheet lived on a laptop, with no fast way to search it from a bookstore aisle.

04 · The Requirements

What we needed

For the third attempt, I used coding agents to translate the photos from attempt two into a custom web app. Building from scratch meant we could also add features the off-the-shelf tools lacked.

  • Bulk import from photos:use image processing to get 1,000 books in fast
  • Search and filter on mobile:fast enough to use mid-bookstore
  • Quick single-book entry:adding a new book should take seconds
  • Per-person read/unread tracking:each household member tracks their own status
  • Find a Book:filter-based mode to surface something to read next from our own shelves
05 · Approach Three · Vibe-Coding

From HTML prototype to a live, deployed app

With coding agents becoming capable enough to handle real infrastructure, I built a custom app from scratch rather than adapting an existing tool.

Claude

HTML prototype with random books

Started with a working proof of concept: search, filter, and a book grid, enough to confirm the idea worked.

Cursor

React app with real features

Turned the prototype into a proper React app. Added bulk editing to mark books as read, and wired up the Open Library API so adding a new book pulls its metadata automatically instead of by hand.

Figma

Visual language and layout

Designed the visual language and layout in Figma before bringing it into code.

Supabase

Database, auth, and deployment

Set up a real database, added authentication, and deployed. Household members share the same book list; each person tracks their own read/unread status.

04b · Feature Highlight

Find a Book

When one of us finishes a book, we browse the shelves together to decide what to read next, filtering by mood, genre, and how much of a commitment we want. Find a Book is a filter-based mode that replicates that process in the app. Each book is enriched with additional metadata that makes the filters more meaningful than title and author alone.

Takeaways

What this project demonstrated

Design for a known user
Precise requirements from lived experience
Two failed attempts produced a specific, well-tested set of requirements before I wrote a line of code. Designing for a problem you've experienced directly removes a lot of ambiguity from the brief.
Full ownership
From requirements to a deployed product
I defined the problem, designed the solution, and shipped a product with real infrastructure: auth, a database, and a live URL. Building what you design gives you a more accurate understanding of the constraints engineers work within.
